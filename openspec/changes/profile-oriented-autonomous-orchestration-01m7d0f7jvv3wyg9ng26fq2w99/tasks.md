# Profile-oriented autonomous orchestration tasks

The design and both delta specs remain the complete acceptance contract. These
four outcome checkpoints replace the unstarted detailed checklist; they do not
remove requirements or grant implementation, Git, profile, or external-write authority.
Before execution, require the design's current successor identity, fresh review,
explicit approval and separate execution authority. Its initial reset/config
recipe is historical only and must not be replayed.
Apply *Minimum outcome task authoring*: this draft's count is not a quota. Each
implementation outcome includes its required tests and documentation; the final
checkpoint checks cross-outcome consistency rather than deferring those obligations.
The splits preserve necessary boundaries: 1 is the pre-source authority/identity
gate; 2 establishes the portable contract required by adapters; 3 has its own
qualification and replacement-readiness gate; 4 independently verifies the integrated candidate.

The orchestrator owns planning/Git/progress and outcome accountability. Qualified
implementers make source, test and implementation-documentation changes, including
review corrections; reviewers remain independent and read-only. All outcomes use
the canonical correction and classification rules without weakening their gates.

## 1. Verify the reconstruction boundary

- [ ] 1.1 Verify the reconstruction boundary and preserve the old profile-preparation inputs without treating them as accepted implementation.

Acceptance: the design's pinned integrated policy base, preserved commits,
historical evidence, successor parent chain, complete inventory and append-only
reconciliation/fresh-review/approval binding verify. Do not treat the successor
parent as integrated, a superseded grant as current approval, or inherited source
as accepted implementation. No reset or config reapplication occurs; local `main`,
source and installed state remain untouched by this verification.
Stop: missing authority, preservation, approval, or identity mismatch.

## 2. Implement the portable core and delivery policies

- [ ] 2.1 Implement and test the agent-neutral orchestration core and `openspec-delivery`/`openspec-next` behavior defined by both delta specs.

Acceptance: focused tests cover minimum-outcome task generation/review, the core,
role ownership, orchestrator-owned correction loop, approach-review and review-record,
and delivery-policy scenarios, including planning-input identity, single-writer
ownership, legacy/default behavior, and operation without Hermes or Herdr.
Verify routine planning/source/validation corrections use the permitted writer
without per-fix approval; material conflicts and exhausted budgets stop, while
planning amendments require fresh review and exact-plan approval before execution.
Goal-aligned contributor-contract and documentation changes are reviewed as part of
the outcome; they do not complete the later package/consistency checkpoint by
themselves and do not require a separate operator checkpoint.
Those working-tree or committed contract edits are inert for the current change through integration and
cannot alter its authority, review, commit, or continuation rules.
Focused tests also cover autonomous turn-budget continuation only after verified
on-track progress against acceptance evidence, one unfinished in-group action,
positive identity readback, and unchanged boundaries. They distinguish a stopping
group/authority budget from a once-only worker iteration-cap continuation; native
prompts, failed gates, drift, contradictory evidence, another stop boundary, or a
second exhaustion of the same action remain non-continuable. Verify the stable
group/native-task/action key survives a fresh session and cannot be reset by
changing a session id or relabeling the same action.
Completion stays blocked by any unsatisfied contract or failed gate. In-scope
repairs use the canonical correction loop; missing authority, non-convergence or
another operator-disposition boundary stops for the operator.

## 3. Deliver qualified terminal workers

- [ ] 3.1 Qualify every candidate in the design, implement only supported adapters, and test role/model configuration, worker supervision, prompt transport, automatic same-session interaction, and legacy-route migration against the orchestration spec.

Acceptance: every candidate has a qualification or unsupported record; at least
one adapter supports implementation and fresh read-only review before any old
route is retired. One-client sequential execution works without requiring distinct
models. Compatible Hermes profile preparation ships only if Hermes qualifies.
Stop: no qualifying implementation/review adapter, an unsafe or unverifiable
boundary, or an unresolved native human-input wait after automatic session exposure.
A native decision resolved through the qualified same-session surface does not
itself fail qualification or terminate the outcome; its result follows the contract.

## 4. Document, package, and verify the candidate

- [ ] 4.1 Verify cross-outcome documentation/routing/test consistency, synchronize final versions and package inventories, run the required gates, and obtain independent review of the exact frozen local candidate.

Acceptance: *Operator configuration documentation* covers every advertised client
and all affected guidance is current; contract edits are independently confirmed to
align with the accepted goal and declared purposes. Retired routes and package contents match
the qualified behavior. Focused suites, `npm run validate:openspec`, `npm test`,
and `npm run build` pass after the final edit, and independent review passes for
the exact candidate SHA. Verify the pre-freeze repository config is unchanged
from the approved planning digest; never defer stale guidance to another change.
Completion stays blocked by failed verification, stale guidance or blocking review;
routine remedies use the canonical correction loop. Stop for an unresolved
operator-disposition boundary or publication, integration, archive, release or
workspace retirement requiring separate authority.
